Method and equations

1Generic cubic equation of state

All four equations are special cases of one form:

$$P=\frac{RT}{V-b}-\frac{a(T)}{(V+\varepsilon b)(V+\sigma b)},\qquad a(T)=\Psi\,\frac{\alpha(T_r,\omega)\,R^2T_c^2}{P_c},\qquad b=\Omega\,\frac{RT_c}{P_c}$$

2PVT: solving for \(Z\)

With \(\beta=\dfrac{bP}{RT}\) and \(q=\dfrac{a}{bRT}\), substituting \(V=ZRT/P\) gives a cubic in \(Z\):

$$(Z-\beta)(Z+\varepsilon\beta)(Z+\sigma\beta)-(Z+\varepsilon\beta)(Z+\sigma\beta)+q\beta\,(Z-\beta)=0$$

For Peng–Robinson, with \(A=\dfrac{aP}{(RT)^2}\) and \(B=\beta\), this is

$$Z^3-(1-B)Z^2+\left(A-3B^2-2B\right)Z-\left(AB-B^2-B^3\right)=0$$

Only real roots with \(Z>\beta\) are physical. The largest is the vapour root, the smallest the liquid root; when both exist, the stable one has the lower fugacity. Then \(V=ZRT/P\).

3Enthalpy and entropy (residual properties)

$$I=\frac{1}{\sigma-\varepsilon}\ln\frac{Z+\sigma\beta}{Z+\varepsilon\beta}\qquad\left(I=\frac{\beta}{Z}\ \text{for vdW}\right)$$ $$\frac{H^R}{RT}=Z-1+\left[\frac{d\ln\alpha}{d\ln T_r}-1\right]qI,\qquad \frac{S^R}{R}=\ln(Z-\beta)+\frac{d\ln\alpha}{d\ln T_r}\,qI$$

where \(\dfrac{d\ln\alpha}{d\ln T_r}=0\) (vdW), \(-\tfrac12\) (RK) and \(\dfrac{-m\sqrt{T_r}}{1+m\left(1-\sqrt{T_r}\right)}\) (SRK, PR). Adding the ideal-gas part, with reference state ideal gas at \(T_0=298.15\) K, \(P_0=1\) bar:

$$H=\int_{T_0}^{T}C_P^{ig}\,dT+H^R,\qquad S=\int_{T_0}^{T}\frac{C_P^{ig}}{T}\,dT-R\ln\frac{P}{P_0}+S^R,\qquad \frac{C_P^{ig}}{R}=A+BT+CT^2+\frac{D}{T^2}$$

4Pure-component fugacity

$$\ln\phi=\int_0^P (Z-1)\frac{dP}{P}=Z-1-\ln(Z-\beta)-qI,\qquad f=\phi P$$

5Saturation pressure

Below \(T_c\), liquid and vapour are in equilibrium when \(f^L=f^V\). Starting from the Wilson estimate, the pressure is updated until the two fugacities agree:

$$P_{\text{new}}=P\,\frac{\phi^L}{\phi^V}\qquad\text{until}\qquad\left|\frac{\phi^L}{\phi^V}-1\right|<10^{-10}$$

Gas data (\(T_c\), \(P_c\), \(\omega\), \(C_P^{ig}\) coefficients): Smith, Van Ness & Abbott, Appendices B and C.
